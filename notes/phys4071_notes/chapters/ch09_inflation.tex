\subsection{Cosmological Puzzles: Flatness Problem}
\begin{intuition}
    Recall the Friedmann equation in energy density form:
    \[
        \Omega_0^{'} - 1 = - \Omega_k = - \frac{k}{a^2 H^2}
    \]
    and it can be further rewritten as:
    \begin{align*}
        | \Omega_0^{'} - 1 | &\propto \begin{cases}
            a^2 & \text{radiation-dominated era} \\
            a & \text{matter-dominated era}
        \end{cases}
    \end{align*}
    which we can see that $| \Omega_0^{'} - 1 |$ grows with time in early universe. \\

    Meanwhile, based on the current measurements by Planck + DESI + SN Ia, we know that $\Omega_k = -0.001 \pm 0.002$ (consistent with zero). This means that the universe is very close to being spatially flat. However, we know that the $\Omega_k$ grows with time in early universe, so it must be extremely close to zero in the early universe; Otherwise, a small deviation from zero in the early universe would have been amplified to a large deviation today, causing our measurements to be inconsistent with the observed flatness. \\

    The $\Lambda$CDM model only directly assume that $\Omega_k$ is close to zero in the early universe as coincidence, but it does not provide a mechanism to explain why $\Omega_k$ is so close to zero. This is known as the \textbf{flatness problem}.
\end{intuition}

\begin{example}{Dark Energy in Flatness Problem}{}
    We know that in dark-energy-dominated era, $| \Omega_0^{'} - 1 |$ decreases with time (i.e. $\propto a^{-2}$), can this help to solve the flatness problem? \\

    \bigskip\textbf{Solution.}\quad \\
    No, it cannot. The reason is that the dark-energy-dominated era only starts at $z \sim 0.3$, which is very late in the history of the universe. But from the observations of CMB, we know that the universe is already very close to being spatially flat at $z \sim 1100$. Therefore, the dark-energy-dominated era cannot help to solve the flatness problem.
\end{example}

\subsection{Cosmological Puzzles: Relic Abundance Problem}
\begin{intuition}
    This problem is model-dependent, and is motivated by the Grand Unified Theories (GUTs) in particle physics. In GUTs, the interactions of the Standard Model (i.e. strong, weak, and electromagnetic interactions) are unified into a single interaction at high energy scales (e.g. $10^{16}$ GeV). When universe cools down from the GUT scale, the unified interaction breaks down into the separate interactions (force) we observe today, and this process is called symmetry breaking. During the symmetry breaking, topological defects such as stable magnetic monopoles can be produced, and their relic abundance can be estimated as:
    \[
        \Omega_{0, \text{monopole}} \sim 10^{11} \cdot \left( \frac{T_{\text{GUT}}}{10^{14} \text{ GeV}} \right)^3 \frac{m_{\text{monopole}}}{10^{16} \text{ GeV}}
    \]
    which is much larger than the observed total matter abundance $\Omega_{0, \text{matter}} \sim 0.3$. Therefore, the GUTs predict a relic abundance of magnetic monopoles that is inconsistent with observations, and this is known as the \textbf{relic abundance problem}.
\end{intuition}
